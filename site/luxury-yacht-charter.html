Luxury Yacht Charter: The Complete Guide

The best of the yachting life for a week, with none of the cost of ownership. Here is everything worth knowing about a luxury yacht charter before you book.

A luxury yacht charter is, for many people, the single best way to experience the yachting life — all of the beauty, freedom, and indulgence of owning a superyacht, for a week or two, with none of the year-round cost or responsibility. You step aboard, a professional crew takes care of everything, and the sea becomes your private playground. Here is everything worth knowing before you book: what a luxury charter includes, what it costs, where to go, and how to choose the right yacht.

What is a luxury yacht charter?

A luxury yacht charter means renting a crewed yacht — typically a superyacht of 80 feet or more — for a defined period, usually by the week. Unlike a simple boat rental, a luxury charter is a fully-serviced experience: the yacht comes with a professional crew including a captain, chef, stewardesses, and deckhands who handle everything from navigation to gourmet meals to water-sports. You decide where you want to go and what you want to do; the crew make it happen. It is the closest thing there is to owning a superyacht without the commitment of ownership.

The appeal is straightforward. Owning a large yacht costs millions to buy and a substantial fraction of that every year to run, as we cover in our guide to yacht ownership costs. A charter lets you enjoy an equivalent vessel for a week, pay only for the time you use, and walk away with no ongoing obligation. For all but the most frequent users, chartering is the more sensible way to access yachts at this level.

What a luxury charter includes

A crewed luxury charter is an all-encompassing experience, though it is important to understand what is and is not in the base price:

  • The yacht and crew — the vessel itself and its full professional crew are the foundation of the charter fee.
  • Accommodation — luxurious cabins, often with en-suite bathrooms, for you and your guests.
  • Service — the crew handle everything: meals, housekeeping, itinerary, water-sports, and anticipating your every need.
  • Water toys and amenities — most luxury yachts carry tenders, jet skis, paddleboards, snorkeling gear, and more.

What typically sits on top of the base charter fee are the running expenses — fuel, food and drink, dockage along the way, and crew gratuity. On most crewed charters these are handled through an Advance Provisioning Allowance, explained below. Understanding this structure up front is the key to avoiding surprises, and it is covered in depth in our guide to what a yacht charter costs.

What does a luxury yacht charter cost?

Luxury charter pricing is usually quoted as a weekly base rate, and it scales with the yacht's size, age, builder, and reputation. A beautiful sailing yacht or a smaller motor yacht can start in the tens of thousands of dollars per week, while a large, modern superyacht runs into the hundreds of thousands, and the most extraordinary mega yachts command over a million dollars per week. On top of the base rate, budget for the running costs — typically delivered as an Advance Provisioning Allowance of around 25–35% of the base fee — plus crew gratuity, customarily in the range of 10–20%.

The All-In Rule

Plan for total charter cost of roughly the base rate plus 30–50% once provisioning, fuel, dockage, and gratuity are included. The headline weekly rate is the start, not the finish.

Where to charter a luxury yacht

Charter destinations follow the seasons. The Mediterranean — the French and Italian Rivieras, the Amalfi Coast, Greece, Croatia, the Balearics — is the summer epicenter of luxury chartering, combining stunning coastlines with glamorous ports. In winter, the fleet migrates to the Caribbean and Bahamas, where turquoise water, secluded coves, and island-hopping define the experience. The Bahamas in particular are an easy reach from Fort Lauderdale, the Yachting Capital of the World, which is why so many charters begin here. Other sought-after grounds include the waters of New England in late summer and increasingly exotic destinations from the South Pacific to Southeast Asia for those seeking something further afield.

How to choose the right luxury yacht

The perfect charter yacht depends on what you want from the trip. Consider the party size — yachts are categorized by how many guests they sleep, and matching the yacht to your group keeps the experience intimate rather than cramped or cavernous. Consider motor versus sail — a motor yacht offers space, speed, and stability, while a sailing yacht offers romance and a more connected experience of the sea. Consider the crew, because on a charter the crew make or break the experience; a great chef and an attentive, professional team turn a good trip into an unforgettable one. And consider the amenities that matter to you, whether that is a particular water toy, a spa, a beach club, or simply a layout that suits how your group likes to relax.

A good charter broker is invaluable here, matching your wishes to the right vessel from a fleet you could never evaluate on your own. The difference between a charter you enjoyed and one you will never forget often comes down to choosing the right yacht in the first place.

The bottom line on luxury yacht charter

A luxury yacht charter delivers the very best of the yachting world — privacy, beauty, indulgence, and total freedom on the water — packaged for a week and freed from the burdens of ownership. Understand the all-in cost, choose the right yacht and crew for your group, pick a destination that fits the season, and you have the makings of one of the great experiences money can buy. For many, a single charter is what sparks a lifelong love of the water — and sometimes the first step toward one day owning a yacht of their own.

Understanding the APA (Advance Provisioning Allowance)

The single most important thing for a first-time charterer to understand is the Advance Provisioning Allowance, or APA. Because a crewed charter's running costs — fuel, food, fine wines, dockage fees, and incidentals — can't be known precisely in advance, the charterer pays an estimated sum up front, typically 25–35% of the base charter rate, into an account the captain manages on your behalf. As the charter unfolds, the captain draws from the APA to cover these real expenses and keeps a transparent accounting. At the end of the trip, you either receive a refund of what wasn't spent or settle any overage.

This system exists because no two charters consume the same resources — a group that cruises hard, dines on lobster and vintage champagne, and docks in marina every night will spend far more than one that anchors in quiet bays and keeps it simple. The APA flexes to match. The practical takeaway: budget for the base rate plus the APA plus gratuity as your true cost, and communicate your preferences to the crew early so they provision to your tastes. Done right, the APA is invisible — you simply enjoy the trip while the captain handles the economics behind the scenes.

Who charters luxury yachts — and why

The luxury charter guest is more varied than the stereotype suggests. Yes, some are ultra-high-net-worth individuals for whom a weekly six-figure charter is routine. But many are successful families pooling resources for a once-in-a-lifetime celebration, groups of friends splitting the cost of a milestone trip, or couples marking a special anniversary in a way no hotel could match. Corporate charters — for client entertainment, executive retreats, or rewards — are common as well. And a meaningful share of charterers are prospective owners, using a charter to test the yachting life before committing to a purchase.

What unites them is the recognition that a yacht offers something no other form of luxury travel can: a private, self-contained world that moves with you, reaches places hotels can't, and adapts entirely to your group. For a special occasion, there is genuinely nothing else like it — which is why so many first-time charterers become repeat charterers, and why some eventually decide that, given how much they love it, ownership might make sense after all.

Motor yacht or sailing yacht charter?

One of the first choices in planning a luxury charter is whether to charter a motor yacht or a sailing yacht, and the two deliver genuinely different experiences. A motor yacht offers generous interior volume, higher speeds to cover more ground, exceptional stability, and the broad beam that makes for spacious decks and large amenities — it is the choice for those who prioritize space, comfort, and the ability to reach more destinations in a week. The majority of luxury charters are aboard motor yachts for exactly these reasons.

A sailing yacht, by contrast, offers something a motor yacht cannot: the romance and quiet of moving under sail, a more intimate connection to the sea and the wind, and access to anchorages and a sailing culture that motor yachts only partly share. Sailing yachts tend to have less interior volume for their length and move more slowly, but for those who love the act of sailing itself — or simply the elegance of it — they are deeply rewarding. Catamarans split the difference, offering the stability and space of two hulls with the economy and feel of sail. There is no wrong answer; the right one depends on whether your priority is the comfort and reach of a motor yacht or the soul and romance of sail.
