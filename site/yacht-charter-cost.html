How Much Does a Yacht Charter Cost?

From a day on the water to a week aboard a superyacht, charter costs span an enormous range. Here is how pricing really works, and the costs first-timers do not see coming.

"How much does a yacht charter cost?" is the first question every prospective charterer asks, and the honest answer is: it depends enormously — on the size and type of yacht, the season, the destination, and how you like to travel. A day on a small boat and a week aboard a superyacht are separated by several orders of magnitude. Here is how charter pricing really works, what drives it, and the costs first-timers don't see coming.

The base charter rate

The headline number in any charter is the base rate — the cost of the yacht and its crew for the charter period. For crewed yachts this is almost always quoted per week, while smaller boats and day charters may be priced per day. The base rate scales with everything that makes a yacht desirable: length, age, builder reputation, condition, crew quality, and amenities. A week aboard a beautiful but modest sailing yacht might start in the tens of thousands of dollars; a large modern superyacht runs into the hundreds of thousands; and the largest mega yachts exceed a million dollars per week, as we cover in our mega yacht charter guide.

The costs beyond the base rate

Here is what first-timers most often miss: the base rate is the beginning, not the total. On a crewed charter, several significant costs sit on top:

  • The APA (Advance Provisioning Allowance) — typically 25–35% of the base rate, paid upfront to cover fuel, food, drink, dockage, and incidentals during the charter. The captain manages it and accounts for it, refunding what isn't spent.
  • Crew gratuity — customary, typically 10–20% of the base rate, given at the end of the charter for good service.
  • Taxes and fees — depending on the cruising area, local taxes (such as VAT in the Mediterranean) can add meaningfully to the cost.
  • Delivery or repositioning fees — sometimes applied if the yacht must travel to meet your itinerary.
The All-In Rule

A realistic total charter budget is the base rate plus roughly 30–50% once the APA, gratuity, and taxes are included. Always plan around the all-in figure, not the headline rate.

What drives charter cost up or down

Several factors move charter pricing, and understanding them helps you find value:

  • Yacht size and type — the single biggest driver. Larger, newer, more prestigious yachts cost more.
  • Season — peak season (Mediterranean summer, Caribbean winter) commands premium rates; shoulder seasons offer better value with often-excellent weather.
  • Motor vs sail — sailing yachts and catamarans are frequently more economical than comparable motor yachts, both in base rate and running costs.
  • Destination — some cruising grounds carry higher costs due to taxes, fees, or sheer demand.
  • Length of charter — weekly rates are standard; longer charters sometimes earn better effective rates.

Day charters vs. weekly charters

Not every charter is a week aboard a superyacht. Day charters — renting a yacht for a single day, often for a celebration, a special outing, or simply a day on the water — are a hugely popular and far more accessible entry point. A day charter on a well-appointed yacht for a group can cost a fraction of a weekly charter and requires far less planning, making it ideal for birthdays, anniversaries, corporate outings, or a first taste of the yachting life. We cover this in detail in our guide to renting a yacht for a day or event. Weekly crewed charters, by contrast, are the immersive experience — multiple days of cruising, dining, and exploring, with the yacht as your private floating home.

How to get the best value

Chartering well is partly about knowing where value hides. Consider shoulder season, when weather is often still excellent but rates and crowds drop. Consider a sailing yacht or catamaran, which deliver a wonderful experience at lower cost than equivalent motor yachts. Be clear with your broker about your true budget so they match you to yachts that fit it all-in, not just on the base rate. And communicate your provisioning preferences thoughtfully — the APA flexes with your tastes, so a group that enjoys simple pleasures will spend less than one demanding the finest of everything every night. A good broker is invaluable in finding the yacht that delivers the most experience for your budget.

The bottom line on charter cost

Yacht charter spans an enormous range, from an accessible day on the water to a seven-figure week aboard a mega yacht. The key to budgeting well is to think in all-in terms — base rate plus APA, gratuity, and taxes — rather than anchoring on the headline figure. Match the yacht, season, and destination to your budget and your group, and chartering delivers extraordinary value: the full yachting experience, precisely when you want it, with none of the cost of ownership. Understood properly, it is one of the smartest ways there is to access the water at the highest level.

Charter cost by yacht size

While exact figures move with season, destination, and the individual yacht, it helps to understand how charter cost scales with size. Smaller crewed yachts and catamarans, in the 40–60 foot range, sit at the accessible end of crewed chartering and are a wonderful entry point for couples and small groups. Mid-size luxury yachts, roughly 60–100 feet, occupy the heart of the market, offering generous space, full crews, and the amenities most charterers imagine, at base rates well into the tens or low hundreds of thousands per week. Large superyachts beyond 100 feet climb into the hundreds of thousands, and mega yachts exceed a million dollars per week, as covered in our mega yacht charter guide. The pattern is consistent: each step up in size brings more space, more crew, more amenities, and a correspondingly higher base rate, with the running costs scaling alongside. Choosing the right size is therefore the single biggest lever on the total cost of your charter, and matching it honestly to your group and your budget is the key to value.
