New vs Used

New or used — which is right for you?

A new yacht depreciates the moment it leaves the dock. But a used one carries its own risks. Here's how to choose the right side of this fork.

Updated June 2026·5 min read

It's the first real fork every yacht buyer faces: a gleaming new boat, or a well-kept used one for a fraction of the price? Both are right answers — for different people.

The case for new

A new yacht is exactly what you want it to be: the latest systems, a full warranty, no previous owner's compromises, and that indescribable feeling of being the first to step aboard. You choose the configuration, the finishes, the electronics. Nothing is worn, nothing is hidden.

The cost of all that is depreciation. Like a new car, a new yacht loses a significant chunk of its value the moment it becomes "used" — the day it leaves the dealer. For the first few years of ownership, you're absorbing that drop.

The case for used

A used yacht lets someone else take the depreciation hit. A boat that's a few years old can cost dramatically less than its new equivalent while being, mechanically, nearly the same vessel. For most first-time buyers, used is the financially smarter entry point — you get more boat for the money, and you're buying after the steepest depreciation has already happened.

The Depreciation Reality

A new yacht's steepest value drop happens in its first few years. Buy a gently-used boat and you let the original owner absorb that — often the single biggest saving available to a buyer.

The catch with used: condition is everything

The risk with used is that a yacht is a complex machine that's been sitting in saltwater. Problems hide. This is why the marine survey is non-negotiable on a used purchase — a professional inspection that tells you what you're really buying. A clean survey on a well-maintained used yacht is one of the best buys in the market. A skipped survey on a neglected one is how people inherit five-figure repair bills.

So which should you choose?

If you value being first, want the newest systems, and the depreciation doesn't trouble you — buy new. If you want the most boat for your money and you're comfortable doing your homework (and getting a thorough survey), used is usually the smarter play, especially for a first yacht. Most experienced owners will tell you the sweet spot is a lightly-used yacht from a quality builder, bought after a clean survey: new enough to be trouble-free, old enough to have skipped the worst of the depreciation.

Whichever you choose, the cost of owning her is similar either way — which is why understanding the true cost of ownership matters more than the new-versus-used question itself.
