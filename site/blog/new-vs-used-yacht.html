New vs Used Yacht: What Fort Lauderdale Buyers Should Know

Should you buy a new or used yacht in Fort Lauderdale? Here's what experienced buyers consider before making one of the most rewarding decisions of their lives.

Updated October 15, 2026

Both new and used yachts can be excellent choices — the right answer depends on your priorities, lifestyle, and how you plan to use the vessel. New yachts offer warranties, modern systems, and the satisfaction of customization. Used yachts offer more boat for the same investment and a known performance record. In Fort Lauderdale, the world's most active yachting market, both paths are well-supported and genuinely compelling.

Should you buy a new or used yacht in Fort Lauderdale?

Fort Lauderdale is perhaps the single best place on earth to answer this question firsthand. The city's miles of marina frontage, its world-famous boat show, and its deep bench of brokers and surveyors mean you can inspect dozens of vessels — new and used — within a single weekend. Buyers here have more options and more professional guidance than virtually anywhere else. Whether you walk a dealer's showroom or explore the brokerage docks at Las Olas or Bahia Mar, you will quickly develop an instinct for what your budget can actually deliver.

Our yacht buying resource center is the best place to start building that instinct before you ever set foot on a dock.

What are the real advantages of buying a new yacht?

A new yacht gives you a clean slate — no previous owner's decisions to live with, no deferred maintenance hiding in the bilge. Factory warranties typically cover the hull, engine, and major systems for defined periods, reducing the financial unpredictability of early ownership. You also get to specify the layout, electronics package, upholstery, and color scheme, which matters enormously when you plan to spend meaningful time aboard.

New vessels built to current standards also incorporate the latest advances in fuel efficiency, stability, and onboard technology. If you plan to take guests offshore regularly or live aboard full-time in Fort Lauderdale's Intracoastal waterways, a new build's reliability can justify the premium. The trade-off is time: popular semi-custom models often have wait lists, and full custom builds from notable yards may require a year or more from contract to splash.

What are the real advantages of buying a used yacht?

Used yachts deliver purchasing power. The same budget that buys a modest new vessel often secures a substantially larger, better-equipped used one — and in Fort Lauderdale's brokerage market, inventory is deep across every category from express cruisers to megayachts. Depreciation has already occurred, which means you are not absorbing the steepest part of the value curve the moment you leave the dock.

A well-maintained used yacht also has a documented history. You can review service records, survey reports, and refit logs to understand exactly what you are buying. Some of the most thoughtfully outfitted vessels on the water are used boats whose owners invested heavily in upgrades — satellite communications, upgraded generators, custom teak work — that would cost far more if ordered new.

Key advantages of buying used, at a glance:

  • More vessel per dollar — larger size or higher specification within the same budget
  • Immediate availability — no build wait, ready to use as soon as the paperwork clears
  • Proven systems — gremlins have often been identified and resolved by the previous owner
  • Negotiating room — brokerage sellers are typically motivated and realistic
  • Known depreciation curve — the steepest value drop has already happened
  • Refit potential — opportunity to personalize to your taste at your own pace

How does the used yacht buying process work in Fort Lauderdale?

In Fort Lauderdale, the brokerage process follows a well-established sequence that protects both buyer and seller. You identify a vessel through a broker, make an offer, and place the vessel under contract with a deposit held in escrow. A sea trial and independent marine survey then take place — the survey is non-negotiable and will examine the hull, machinery, electrical systems, safety equipment, and structural integrity in detail. Based on survey findings, the parties negotiate any adjustments before closing.

For new vessels purchased through a dealer or direct from a builder, the process is more similar to a custom order. You will review a detailed specification sheet, select options, and pay in staged installments tied to construction milestones. Either path benefits enormously from local expertise — a Fort Lauderdale broker who knows the waterways, the yards, and the common issues with specific vessel types is among the most valuable resources a buyer can have.

What should you inspect before buying a used yacht in Fort Lauderdale?

Every used vessel deserves a thorough professional survey — full stop. Beyond that, buyers who educate themselves before the survey tend to ask better questions and catch more issues. During your initial inspection, pay attention to:

  1. Engine room condition — cleanliness and organization reveal how previous owners cared for the vessel overall
  2. Bilge water — any standing water or oil residue warrants further investigation
  3. Deck hardware and seals — Fort Lauderdale's sun and salt environment accelerate wear on hatches, cleats, and through-hulls
  4. Electronics and navigation systems — assess what will need updating and factor that into your offer
  5. Upholstery and interior joinery — cosmetic work is manageable but adds up quickly
  6. Logs and service records — a well-documented history is a sign of a well-managed boat

A professional marine surveyor will go deeper than any of the above, but arriving informed puts you in a far stronger position.

How does Fort Lauderdale's market compare to other cities for yacht buyers?

Fort Lauderdale's market is simply without peer in North America for breadth and liquidity. The Fort Lauderdale International Boat Show — the largest in the world — draws buyers, sellers, builders, and brokers from every major yacht-producing country. Year-round, the city's marinas and brokerage yards hold inventory that other cities see only at shows. This concentration of supply means buyers have genuine choice and genuine leverage.

The city's marine services infrastructure is equally impressive. Surveyors, sea trial captains, refit yards, and warranty service centers are all within easy reach, which makes the due diligence phase of a purchase faster and more thorough than it would be in smaller markets. Whether you are buying a 40-foot cruiser or a 100-foot motoryacht, Fort Lauderdale can support the entire transaction from search to splash.

For those considering the flexibility of access without full ownership, yacht chartering is worth exploring before you commit — and it is something Fort Lauderdale does exceptionally well.

A used yacht typically sells for significantly less than an equivalent new model, though the gap depends on age, condition, brand, and market demand. Vessels a few years old in excellent condition can offer strong value, while heavily used boats may require costly refits that close the price gap. Always factor in a professional survey before committing.
A marine survey is a detailed inspection of a vessel's structure, systems, and condition performed by a certified marine surveyor. It is essential when buying any used yacht and is typically required before a sale can be completed. It protects you from hidden defects and gives you negotiating power.
New yachts often qualify for straightforward coverage because their condition and systems are factory-certified, which insurers tend to favor. Older vessels may require a recent survey to obtain coverage and could carry higher premiums depending on age and materials. Either way, working with a marine insurance specialist is advisable.
