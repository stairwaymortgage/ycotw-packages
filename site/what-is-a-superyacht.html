What Is a Superyacht?

The gleaming giants that define the world's glamorous harbors. Here is what a superyacht actually is, how big, what it costs, and what it takes to own or charter one.

Few words in yachting carry the glamour of "superyacht." These are the gleaming giants that define the harbors of Monaco, Fort Lauderdale, and the French Riviera — but what is a superyacht, exactly? How big does a yacht have to be to earn the name, what sets these vessels apart, and what does it take to own or charter one? Here's a complete guide to the most aspirational class of yacht.

What is a superyacht?

A superyacht is a large, luxurious yacht — generally 80 feet (about 24 meters) or more — that is big enough to require professional crew and to offer serious onboard luxury. There's no single official cutoff, but 80 feet is the commonly-cited threshold where a yacht crosses from a large private vessel into the superyacht class. Above this size, yachts feature professional crew, multiple staterooms, generous amenities, and the kind of finish and engineering associated with the very top of the market. The largest superyachts shade into the mega yacht category, typically used for vessels over 200 feet.

What sets a superyacht apart

Beyond sheer size, several things define a superyacht:

  • Professional crew — superyachts are run by trained professional crews, from a captain and engineer to chefs, stewardesses, and deckhands.
  • Luxury and amenities — staterooms, multiple decks, often pools, jacuzzis, gyms, beach clubs, and an array of water toys.
  • Engineering and build quality — superyachts are sophisticated vessels built by elite shipyards, often largely custom.
  • Range and capability — many can cross oceans, reaching distant and exclusive destinations in comfort.

The result is less a boat than a private, mobile luxury residence capable of taking its owner and guests anywhere in extraordinary comfort.

What does a superyacht cost?

Superyachts begin around $20 million for a 100-foot vessel and climb into the hundreds of millions for the largest and most bespoke builds. And the purchase price is only the beginning: annual running costs follow the rule of thumb of roughly 10% of value per year, driven heavily by crew and fuel — meaning a superyacht can cost millions annually just to operate. We cover the full picture in our guide to yacht costs. For most people, chartering a superyacht for a week is the far more accessible way to experience this level of yachting.

Who owns superyachts?

Superyacht owners are among the world's wealthiest individuals — business magnates, entrepreneurs, and family dynasties for whom a superyacht represents both the ultimate private retreat and a statement of success. Famous owners and their vessels capture public fascination, from tech billionaires to industrialists. For these owners, a superyacht offers something no other possession can: complete privacy, total freedom of movement, and a self-contained world of luxury that travels with them. Many superyachts are also chartered out when the owner isn't using them, helping offset the substantial running costs.

The superyacht experience

Whether you own or charter, time aboard a superyacht is defined by the extraordinary ratio of service and amenity to guest. A professional crew anticipates every need, world-class meals appear effortlessly, and any activity imaginable — from diving off the swim platform to a beach setup on a remote island — is simply arranged. The yacht can carry you to a different breathtaking anchorage each day while you want for nothing. It is, by almost any measure, the most luxurious way to experience the sea, and the reason superyachts hold such an enduring place in the imagination.

The bottom line on superyachts

A superyacht is a large luxury yacht, generally 80 feet and up, crewed by professionals and built for the highest level of comfort and capability on the water. Owning one is the province of the very wealthy, but chartering one places the experience within reach of far more people for a week of their lives. Either way, the superyacht represents the summit of yachting — the vessels that define the world's most glamorous harbors, including those of Fort Lauderdale, the Yachting Capital of the World.

Superyacht vs. mega yacht vs. gigayacht

As you explore the upper reaches of yachting, you'll encounter a hierarchy of terms, and while none are rigidly official, they describe meaningful steps up in scale. A superyacht generally starts around 80 feet and runs up into the low hundreds of feet. A mega yacht describes the larger vessels within this world, typically those over about 200 feet, with the size, crew, and amenities to match. At the very top, the term gigayacht is sometimes used for the true giants exceeding roughly 300 feet — the handful of vessels that approach the scale of small cruise ships and represent the absolute pinnacle of private shipbuilding. The boundaries between these terms are fuzzy and used loosely, but the progression is real: each step up brings dramatically more space, larger crews, greater range, and exponentially higher costs. When someone refers to a superyacht versus a mega yacht, they're signaling roughly where on this extraordinary spectrum a particular vessel sits.

The world of superyacht builders

Part of what defines a superyacht is who built it. The superyacht world revolves around a select group of elite shipyards, many in Europe, whose names carry the same prestige among yacht owners that the finest marques carry among car collectors. These yards build largely custom vessels, where an owner commissions a yacht designed to their exact specifications over a process that can take years — selecting the naval architect, the interior designer, the layout, and countless bespoke details. This is part of why superyachts cost what they do: each is often a one-of-a-kind creation, engineered and crafted to a standard few objects on earth match. A superyacht's builder and designer strongly influence its value, its reputation, and its desirability on the resale and charter markets, much as a building's architect shapes its standing. Understanding that a superyacht is typically a bespoke, custom-built creation rather than a production product is key to understanding both its cost and its allure.

Life and crew aboard a superyacht

A defining feature of the superyacht, and one that distinguishes it sharply from smaller yachts, is that it operates with a full professional crew who live and work aboard. This crew is what transforms a superyacht from a vessel into a fully-serviced floating residence. The captain bears ultimate responsibility for the yacht, its safety, and its operation; the engineer keeps the sophisticated systems running flawlessly; the interior crew, led by a chief stewardess, deliver service to a standard rivaling the finest hotels; the deck crew handle the yacht's exterior, its tenders, and its water toys; and a professional chef provides cuisine to match. For the owner and guests, this means stepping aboard to find everything anticipated and cared for — a level of seamless service that's central to the superyacht experience and simply isn't possible on a vessel small enough to operate yourself. The crew are skilled professionals pursuing careers in yachting, and a great crew, retained over years, becomes part of what makes a particular superyacht special.
